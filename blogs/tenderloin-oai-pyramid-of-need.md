## The Context

In my previous article [it's all there](/blogs/its-all-there.md),
I claimed that,
there's rarely completely new knowledge,
mostly just new combination of them to applied new contexts,
which make them super-interesting nevertheless.
Much knowledge is already in the books or internet,
but it's one thing to be aware of something,
it's another to actually understand something,
it's totally something else to be able to experience something related,
and actually connecting the experience to the abstract concept model.

So, here's one out of many, many examples I've been thinking about recently:
pyramid of needs.

Why I've been thinking about it?

As a summer intern on ChatGPT,
many of the peers are very ambitious, talented, and accomplished for our age,
and a super frequent conversation them is: what are you doing next?
Are you chasing to become an AI researcher or joining a quantitative trading firm to make the big bucks (cash or paper money)?
Are you trying to stay in the current role as an engineer and try to have a bigger impact and leadereship roles?
And then what? See [The Rat Race](/blogs/rat-race.md) for full context.

And then I thought about Maslov's psychological definition of Pyramid of Needs.
Well, that helped answer some uncertainty in my thinking, at least it provided a structure to my thinking.
And I realized how my summer interning at OpenAI is able to touch some of the lowest parts of the pyramid of needs
all the way up to the abstract parts.

Along the way, I will also throw in some examples from my high school and college days in general.

## My Summer and The Pyramid of Need

This is the iconic diagram for pyramid of needs:

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/60/Maslow%27s_Hierarchy_of_Needs.svg/1920px-Maslow%27s_Hierarchy_of_Needs.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=imageinfo&utm_content=thumbnail)

And Wikipedia adds that, "In his later writings, Maslow added a sixth level of "meta-needs" and meta-motivation".

By definition of a pyramid,
whatever is at a lower level would appear to the boring and unnoticed,
but,
when they are damanged or worse if removed completely,
upper layers of the pyramid would crumble and that's when you start to appreciate a cornerstone's importance.

My summer somehow illustrated this pyramid pretty thoroughly, so, that's a curious case study.

### Physiological

Breathing, homeostasis, and excretion-wise,
thankfully,
my summer has been fine.
Usually these are only damaged when one's sick.
which I haven't bumped into this summer yet but certainly bumped into it every quarter at UCSB
(people get sick in college so. so. frequently. omg.),
and I've seen friends getting sick for various reasons and symptoms this summer or in life in general.

And yeah I agree.
When one is healthy,
you wouldn't think about it at all.
You only notice it when it goes wrong.
When you are 100 Fahrenheit in bed swallowing Tylenol's,
the only two things you would pray for are "please make all sickness go away and why does disease exist in the first place"
and "oh gosh I'm gonna miss that school / work deadline and I'm fucked for return offer or bonus or promotion."

There's a reason people say "prioritize health first" because if you don't you just die and nothing else happens.

Food and water wise,
I realized just how brittle I am (more like, how every human is).
If it's a work day and I'm at the office, I'm saved by free cafeteria food and drinks.
But if it's at night at home or on the weekend, I start to appreciate and understand the importance of water and food security as basic needs.
If I got lost scrolling or playing video games on the weekend and eat some hours late,
my body just start shivering and I actually feel my activity is slowing down;
but thankfully all it takes to realive me is a hamberger and then I'm up and running again.

For water, I'm basically dependent on my friend and another fellow intern and neighbor Sam Wang's britta filter bottle;
whenever he's not home, I start to panic a bit and have to go on an Odyssey across the tenderloin street to buy plastic bottled water half a mile away.

Sleep-wise, oh wow, I should write an essay on this by itself.

For me,
if I don't sleep more than 8 hours,
even 30 minutes less,
I would just wake up and be a zombie for the rest of the day.
My head would think at reduced capacity,
and I would get very moody,
and all sorts of things that happen in the higher levels of pyramid of need just won't happen because the cornerstone of sleep is missing.
When I sleep for any minute more than 8 hours though,
holy,
I really do see the world as sunshine rainbow and lolipop even though there's 6 or 7 things burning down that day.
This meme describes me on happy days pretty well:

![](https://s.yimg.com/lo/mysterio/api/88663b4cd37205acd2bbb6a9f43e2456d6805491aff7afb99e01a7d2b44c65cf/lightyear_networkapi/resizefill_w485_h477%3Bquality_80%3Bformat_webp/https%3A%2F%2Fmedia.zenfs.com%2Fen%2Fknow_your_meme_909%2Fb5aceee2712d9c0c50378634ea3d16df)

I believe gen z is at a sleep deprivation crisis.
So many college students around my age are living with 5-6 hours of sleep per day and that's just horrible.
I see so many people, across well-accomplished and not well accomplished alike,
sleeping at 3am on some days and 6am other days and waking up feeling shit couple hours later.
Every news outlet is talking about AI, finance, entertainment, and other stuff,
and there's mental health on the headline too.
But I'm somewhat confident (but don't quote me clinically on this) that if every zen Z in this world is blessed with 8 hours of sleep,
likely many can be happier; at least it's a net positive.

It's a privilege to have good sleep.
I have a few friends with chronic sleep problems.
Their eyes always seem half open and their words sound feeble,
and I feel sad for them.
Imagine how much cooler and happier and productive and amazing they can be if they can also sleep 8 hours a day.
