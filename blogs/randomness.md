## Another Essay on Randomness

I'm back to blogging!

New observations and experiences make way for new exclamation and thinking.
And by Claude Shannon's definition that "information is surprise",
and by definition of a good essay must be full of helpful information,
surprises create material for good essay,
and surprises come from new observations and experiences.
So, new observations and experiences make new essays.

What new experiences and observations of mine made me write this essay?

1. OpenAI summer intern return offers and other gossips I heard
2. My full-time SWE recruiting processes
3. Hearing a few peers' full-time recruiting SWE processes, or just life and career lores in general

Two of my favorite essays,
[Extreme Variance](/blogs/the-rng-of-college-admission.md)
and [The Cardboard Boat Race](/blogs/the-cardboard-boat-race.md),
were already themed on randomness.
_Extreme Variance_ mostly enumerates my observation on the variance of college-admission outcome,
while _The Cardboard Boat Race_ goes beyond my failure with the cardboard boat race
and try to conjecture some techniques to deal with randomness.

This new essay, _randomness_,
builds upon the same structure.
It is a further, more updated lamentation and exclamation of randomness in life.
And more importantly,
I will try to think and list out some ways to hedge against it.

Here we go.
