## Foreword

As I said in my blog's overview paragraph,
I would only "write about events that happened at least a year ago"
because I can't write about ongoing events due to their volatile nature.

So, here's something that mattered to me a lot back in the days.
It was Cupertino High School (CHS) Associated Student Body (ASB).
Aka the student government of CHS. Cupertino ASB, just like the student government in almost every school,
is tasked with organizing school dances, rallies, club fairs,
basically everything school-wide events related.

Compared to the ASB in other schools,
Cupertino ASB got things done pretty well, events were all organized timely and nicely.
However, we did suffer the sins almost every ASB suffered:
nepotism, uneven distribution of work, and lack of innovation.
The majority of ASB officers got in because they were in the cool-kids league with the ASB President / VP,
so if you are not one of them, you naturally get singled out.
There's no rewards or punishments for doing a lot or little work,
so usually for each team within ASB, one person is carrying that team and the rest just ride along,
and it would be a blessing if those riding along simple doesn't cause any trouble or roadblocks.
Finally, for any proposal trying to change to the existing system or simply trying something new,
you would go up to the podium, introduce your proposal, and get bombarded with a billion "_it's great, but..._"

I suffered all three vices.
I wasn't in the cool-kids league.
I tried to get things done, so I had to carry the team many times.
And I tried to initiate a lot of innovations, and, as a result, got hit with many "_it's great, but..._".


Still, I loved Cupertino ASB.
It was a place where many hours of the best years of my teenage days went, and teenage days were precious.
It was a place that fueled so much of my growth in leadership and innovation.
It was I place I called home.

Let me write the lore down before I eventually forget it all.
There's not that many precious memories one would be able to have,
so you gotta hang on tight to your memories.

By the way, my lore with Cupertino ASB intersected with my journey of founding Tino News Today (TNT),
the beloved (I think lol) newscast program that everyone watches at CHS nowadays.
So, this article will touch on TNT too.

Actually, it all started with TNT.

## My Very First Attempt to TNT

My family just moved from Maryland to California by loading all of our belongings into our Toyota Camry
and driving it across the country.
For whatever reason, whether you call it ego or self-drive,
I was determined to do something big and make a hit at Cupertino High School.

The middle school I went to in Maryland, Robert Frost Middle School,
had a TV newscast program where kids would anchor the show to broadcast announcement bulletins, worldwide news, and other types of content.

I figured that CHS didn't have something like that.
If it worked in Robert Frost, surely it would work at CHS.
CHS did have morning announcements though, but it was purely audio via the PA speakers.

So, literally on the 3rd day of my arrival at CHS,
I went to the ASB teacher (ASB runs the morning announcements) and told him about my idea to make video announcements.

And of course he said no.

## My Failed Club Application

I didn't give up my plan to make something big though.

As a confused freshman figuring out what to do,
I sat in a couple club meetings and became determined that,
existing clubs were too boring: they are nothing but Google Slides presentations.
I especially remembered the FBLA (business club's) presentation: it was so boring.
The monotone and figures accurately reflected how boring being a business-related major or job might look like.

So I decided to create a club that's actually fun.
How about, a Minecraft Club?
People loved playing Minecraft.
The irony was: I don't even play Minecraft.
Nowadays, whenever I try a new initiative,
I would make extra sure I first have the qualifications to make that initiative happen.
But the younger version of myself back in Minecraft Club days was too ignorant I guess.
I applied to form a new Minecraft Club anyways.

Of course Minecraft Club was rejected by ASB.
Specifically, rejected by the same teacher who rejected my video announcements proposal,
as well as the three ASB Club Commissioners who are like the overlords of everything club-related at CHS.

One of the Club Commissioner was the President of FBLA who bored me out earlier with the boring presentations,
he's also President of Tinovation, and now also he's off to OpenAI too.
Besides the FBLA part, I basically followed his foot steps,
as I later as well became a Club Commissioner, served as Executive Officer for Tinovation, and now off to OpenAI 😂.
Small world.

## My Failed Election

Well, video announcements didn't work, founding Minecraft club didn't work,
maybe I can try running for ASB student council next.
After all, I gotta keep on trying.

So, I ran for the lowest position in the student council: rally chair.
But apparently many others thought running for a lower position is easier to get in,
which it turned out not, as rally chair became the most contested position with 6 candidates whereas President only had 1 or 2.

Obviously, for someone who just drove all the way from Maryland to California,
with no friends and popularity, I lost the election.

## Giving Up

I really felt like giving up. I felt that every door was closed to me.
In hindsight, I was simply too pompous to be able to get anywhere.
You need to be humble and down-to-earth to start at the bottom and work it up.

Nevertheless, I gave another shot to Minecraft Club.
The ASB Teacher told me that, there's already a gaming club on campus.
So, from his suggestion, I asked the gaming club officer if I can start a Minecraft division under gaming club,
so that I can carry out events as planned.

Gaming Club officers said yes, so Minecraft Division hosted its grand opening,
attracted 120 attendance in its first meeting (the largest club meeting ever recorded),
but within weeks died down to 10 routine participants,
ran into huge ideological fights with Gaming Club officers on Discord,
got the ASB Teacher and Assistant Principal involved in the squabble,
and was shut down.

I gave up.

## COVID, Reading, and Learning

COVID came;
as everything around my life changed to the virus and quarantine,
all of a sudden, I wasn't too distressed about the video announcements, Minecraft, or setback as much anymore.

I forgot who told me to do this, but I started reading biographies of famous people like crazy.
Steve Jobs, Benjamin Franklin, Dwight Eisenhower, Henry Kissinger, to name a few.
I barely remembered the details of the plots,
but the images of great men with courage, self-drive, innovation, and integrity became deeply imprinted in my head,
and I wish I could be like them one day.

I also picked up the habit of reading leadership books.
I started with the infamous, and very-much "self-help" ish book _48 Laws of Power_,
which from my experience I later concluded that real leadership must do everything the opposite of what _48 Laws of Power_ advocated,
because _48 Laws of Power_ is simply too Machiavellian.
Then I read the good books, from the legendary UCLA Basketball Coach John Wooden's _Wooden On Leadership_ to _How Google Works_ by its ex-CEO Eric Schmidt.
These books shaped my style of leadership: always be kind and fair, and be strict in necessary times.

During COVID, I also found my passion for coding. See [My Tech Growth](/blogs/my-tech-growth.md) for more.

The wisdoms I gained from the biographies and leadership books became critical
to the founding of Tino News Today
and have continued to power my leadership principals to this day.
And, for the years passed since freshman year of high school,
I've read even more, applied them even more, corrected my belief from errors,
and continue to practice this feedback loop.

## Preparing for TNT

I _really_ thought I deserved to shoot another shot at Tino News Today.
After all, newscasting worked in my middle school in Maryland,
and it makes sense to do announcements video style because audio style is too unengaging to be effective.

My first attempt to pitch TNT on my 3rd day of school failed because I was not ill prepared.
Sure, I had a vision, but I had not thought about the implementation of the vision at all.
I didn't think about what exactly should each episode contain,
how the production workflow for the episodes would be organized,
and how the organization to power the production would be recruited and lead.

So, using the leadership and management ideas as I read them from the books,
I drew out some organizational charts, production workflow graphs,
and meticulous planned out the episodes content layouts on the Google Docs.

It was still quarantine, I didn't know when school will return in-person so that I can attempt TNT again,
and I knew very well that even if I try again, I very likely would fail.
Nevertheless, I was on a mission to do something big, and I was determined to make it happen.

## My Successful Club Application, Kind Of

Meanwhile, a friend of mine approached me to help him Co-Found a Table Tennis club.
The club would be functional after quarantine, he argued.
He lacked experience navigating the ASB paperworks,
and since I battled ASB with Minecraft Club's founding and arguments,
he invited me to Co-Found the Table Tennis Club and navigate the application paperwork.
Once again, much like the irony to founding Minecraft Club,
I didn't even play Table Tennis...

The New Club Application Process only accepts 5 out of 30 to 40 applicants every year.
I knew the chance were slim, but the downside is small.
So he filled out the application, I gave some advice along the way,
and I was shocked and happy to see Table Tennis Club getting approved.

That was the first time at CHS I succeeded in something;
even though I was a secondary if not minor character in contribution,
it was a great boost to my confidence and self-drive
as I thought earlier that all the doors were closed to me.

Later on, I served as VP to Table Tennis Club,
helped to host the weekly practice events, secured our own table, and fulfilled my duty
despite not actually playing table tennis myself.
The President went on to become the President of FBLA. Small world.

## What? How? I'm In I guess

Towards the end of Sophomore year,
I got an email in my school inbox about applying for ASB Commissioners.
Despite having annoyed the ASB Teacher with my daring attempt to start video announcements,
annoyed the Club Commissioners with my stupid Minecraft Club Application,
and annoyed the Assistant Principal and the ASB Teacher and Club Commissioners
with my drama for Minecraft Division vs. its parent Gaming Club,
I dropped my resume to apply to be a — how dare I — Club Commissioner.
I guess when you are an unknown little character,
you just need to put your face aside and shoot your shot at whatever opportunity you can try for.

I knew I would be competing against the cool kids club who were already friends with the ASB President since elementary school.
I knew I would be competing against much more popular and competent candidates who were already very experienced with student leadership.
But I didn't blink.
I went into the interview fully prepared with my plan on what I want to accomplish as a Clubs Commissioner.
I told them what was wrong with the new clubs application process,
what was wrong with the clubs rulesbook and enforcement,
and proposed my vision for a simpler new clubs application process,
cleaner clubs rulesbook and enforcement, and more.
I wouldn't have been able to think strategically and tactically well had I not read the biographies and leadership books during quarantine.

A week later, I got the "You Are In" email.
Nowadays, I am perceived by friends as a very competent guy in leadership and software engineering,
but back then, I didn't have that much experience on my resume really.
Minecraft was a flop, Table Tennis Club hasn't begun operating,
yes I've read the leadership books, but I haven't taken on any concrete leadership endeavors
when I received my appointment to serve as Clubs Commissioner.

Holy crap. I am now the Clubs Commissioner.
I would be sitting on the other side of the table that the overlords of clubs would sit.
I would now serve for the very same ASB Teacher that probably despised me for all the reasons.
The Gaming Club officers who looked me down all of a sudden offered me the olive branch.
I finally understood how Imposter Syndrome feels like. I shouldn't be there.

I don't want to be an imposter. I must pick up more skills, pour in my heart and soul,
and just do as best as I can to prove myself that I can be competent and be a good Clubs Commissioner.
Eventually, I shined in my role as Clubs Commissioner and later in founding Tino News Today.
But for the first half of my journey with Cupertino ASB, it was a socially and politically rough water.
