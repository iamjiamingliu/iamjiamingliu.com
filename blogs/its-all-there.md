In a recent biology class, the professor told us, very coarsely paraphrased:

"Darwin didn't invent the idea of evolution out of nowhere.
He built upon his grandfather's idea of adaptation and evolution,
built upon other philosophers' interpretation on animal evolvement,
and compiled everything together with his own observations and conclusions too.
He was afraid of presenting what he found until very later in life;
when he did finally present to the London scientists,
another renowned scientist in the audience mused,
'wow, it's been there all along. There's the age-old idea of adaptation, there's natural selection, and there's speciation...
how come we never thought about putting everything together this way before? It's been here all along."

I look around things I've experienced or heard
and couldn't help but think,
wow,
it's been here all along.
Much knowledge has been in books forever,
much human experience has been there forever.
There are very few GPT or steam engine magnitude breakthroughs in history
that come along and changes everything.
But even these changes come from new combinations plus incremental improvements of existing ideas that suddenly became so powerful.

Most of the things we come across are already out there,
we just need to be aware that they exist,
know how they work to a reasonable extent,
know when or when not to apply something,
and know how to combine things to make a powerful blow in new scenarios.

Most of the human experiences and advices are already there on the internet or books.
The problem is, many experiences listed are exaggerated or unrepresentative of the population,
but still, they are already there.
Whenever I encounter a new experience that throws off my understanding of the world and emotions a bit,
I try to connect the dots to words I've heard before,
and many times they do.
But still,
it's one thing to hear about something,
it's hits way deeper when one experiences it first hand.
Nevertheless, one's experience would likely already be captured somewhere on the internet.

Most of the knowledge are certainly already there.
Even in a domain like tech,
you look around concepts and frameworks that seem really fancy and new,
if you just look a little more carefully,
it very likely turns out,
somebody in the 1980s or earlier Xerox PARC was also facing analogous problems
and came up with solutions that still carry over today.

But the scenarios change.
The people change,
the environment change,
so,
we need to always add twists to existing solutions to make things work today.
And new connections are made between dots,
and, much rarely, actually meaningful dots are discovered,
and all of a sudden we are back to the drawing board rethinking many things.

We are in a forever evolution,
in terms of knowledge,
in terms of experience,
and in terms of humanity.
Many things have been here all along,
but they can always be recombined,
introduced with new dots,
and the outcome would shine totally different.
