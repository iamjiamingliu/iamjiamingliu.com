Every once in a while, we ask ourselves: "what are we fucking doing with our lives? What do we really want? Where do we wanna go next in life?"
Especially when we are in a new environment, in the middle of setbacks, or in a competitive environment,
we seem to be more prone to ask this existential question.
And then we gradually forget about this question as life goes on,
but this question would eventually resurface.

So, what's the answer to this existential question?

There is no answer.
But, I found a pretty good framework for one to find a heuristic for what they want to do in life:
the pyramid of needs.
Yup, it's an age-old concept that's [been here all along](/blogs/its-all-there.md),
but in the age of AI and software engineering changing faster than ever,
or just modern 2026 life changing faster than ever,
I'm pretty sure people today need to answer "what do we really want in life" more than ever,
and pyramid of needs is at least one good framework for reference.

Here it is, the pyramid of needs:

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/60/Maslow%27s_Hierarchy_of_Needs.svg/1920px-Maslow%27s_Hierarchy_of_Needs.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=imageinfo&utm_content=thumbnail)

By definition of a pyramid,
it means the top layer is likely one's ultimate call in life,
but every layer below recursively provide the foundation to the level about,
for without that layer or a compromised layer, all upper layers will be compromised.

This means by the pyramid of needs definition,
one's ultimate call in life is to live virtuously and morally,
be creative, be spontaneous, be fair, and embrace the facts of the world.
Well, these definitions are somewhat abstract,
and everyone would have a different definition and implementation of what it means to be moral and creative.
Will circle back to this later in the essay.

The Pyramid of needs become really concrete in its lower layers though,
as they directly answer an equally important counterpart to the existential question of "what do we want in life":
what do we _not_ want in life.

Starting at the lowest level of the pyramid is our physiological needs.
When do not want to get sick,
we do not want to get insomnia,
and we do not want to get hungry and thirsty.
These principles sound as simple and foundational as they are,
but how many of us can perfectly follow them?
Take sleep as the biggest example.
