I interned at ChatGPT this summer.
As of July 2026, OpenAI is one of the most spotlighted institutions by the media, with love and hate.
I open up my phone and see traditional media and social media glazing or hating OpenAI for whatever reason,
and then I go to the office, see what code is in Github and what slides are presented in meetings,
and I couldn't help but realize, gosh, whatever the media is spotlighting, it's so shallow.

"Company X is IPO'ing for xyz amount of money"

"Company Y is developing the most capable AI model ever"

"Company Z is considering laying off 10% of its workforce"

"Company A is off to its greatest revenue year ahead"

"Company B is having a new CEO, promising change"

"Company C is fighting a supreme court lawsuit, arguing for xyz reason"

If you zoom out to a 10-year time horizon,
or even a 40-year time horizon,
media headlines always revolve these clichés.
Sure, it's not misinformation, but it's so shallow, and it's missing so much other important information.
To make things worst,
media rarely come back to the same topic to show its trend over time,
because by that time, the trend would have faded.

Seems like, no one cares about the whole truth over time anymore.
After all, why would people care?
Our lives are already busy enough,
and media's purpose, unfortunately, has degenerated into ragebait entertainment instead of genuine deep facts and analysis.

It's not just the media, but information on the internet as a whole.
Sure, I love all the technical blogs teaching me how to get hands on xyz,
but there's so little information on anything beyond hello world.
Even in a lot of official documentations, the examples for more advanced features are very much hello worlds.
The problem is even worse for system design or anything in combination of several moving pieces.
Sure, `bytebytego` and `github.com/system-design-primer` are cool,
but there's so much in between "we can scale a database by sharding" to actually using a distributed database,
or actually scaling a database.

And technical knowledge is just one tiny example.
Think about job offers, company missions, team cultures
-- these information simply do not accurately exist on the internet.
And let's zoom out even more, not just tech or jobs.
Any experience or knowledge that are somewhat more niche than hello world, is not on the internet.

One needs to know, repeatedly practice, and reflect on an insight before understood enough to share it with others.
But if one knows 50% of some knowledge, they might only be able to accurate write down and share 10% of that.
The deeper knowledge goes, the less people know deep enough to share them,
and those people who know enough to share are simply too busy to be a LinkedIn warrior to scream,
and it just takes a huge amount of time to share deep knowlege in the first place.
As a result, most of the information online is large in quantity and shallow in depth.
Much of the good juices are latent in people's minds or only exchanged with good friends on good days.
Whatever is visible, is just the tip of an iceburg.

So, having friends is helpful.
Because very fast, Google and ChatGPT stop working accurately,
and word of mouth from trusted sources really start to kick in.
If the whole point of having friends is to gather intelligence,
then that's just an absolute shame,
but you have to admit that,
何为好友呢？

子曰，“友直、友谅、友多闻。”

It roughly translates to,
"good friends know ball, type shit."

LOL.
