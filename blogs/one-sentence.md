## So You Want to Start a Club

You are applying to create a new club at Cupertino High School.
Because you care about coding / gardening / guitar / your culture and ethnicity / pickle ball / whatever.
Just kidding, you of course don't.
You are applying to create a new club to write on your college app and shut it down once you graduate.

Regardless of your intention,
you go write up a meticulously planned written application.
Back when I was in high school there was no ChatGPT, but I assume it would be very ChatGPT generated now.
You rally your time convincing a teacher to sponsor your club and friends to serve as your officers.

Then you pray your application can make it to the interview step.
If you luckily do,
then you spend hours rehearsing yourself,
and maybe your friends (sorry, I meant fellow officers),
speaking in front of the mirror.

If you luckily make it to the final round,
you likely would have spent dozens if not several dozens of hours already on trying to add one more activity to your college app.
Sorry, I meant founding a new club that you definitely care about.

And you think the Clubs Commissioners would meticulously spend a lot of time and due diligence on evaluating your application?
They claim so,
but of course they don't!
They are just some other high school kids,
as dumb as you if not dumber,
as lazy as you if not lazier.
who happen to be the bestie of the ASB (student government) President since elementary school and earned their titles that way.

Your hard work,
hours writing up application,
hours rallying around persuading teacher and friends,
hours speaking in front of the mirror to prepare for the interview,
become a few sentences of "this is good" or "this is bad"
with at most a few minutes of verbal discussions
for club commissioners.

How do I know this?
Because I spent my first 2 years trying to create new clubs at Cupertino High School,
then my last 2 years sitting on the other side of the table serving as ASB Clubs Commissioner.
And no I didn't know the ASB President since kid, I was an outsider.
I tried to meticulously think through each application,
argue for cases,
but some of my peers' inaction or frivolousness really made me sad for the matrix.

## The Asymmetry

I'm about to graduate college and enter the workforce now,
but I still like to recount examples from things I did in high school because,
some universal principles seem to exist no matter where you are and no matter if you are dealing with kids or adults.
Adults are just kids born a few years earlier.

In this case, the principle seems to be that,
there's such a significant asymmetry in power and diligence between those asking and those approving.
It could be high school new club applications.
It could be college admission applications.
It could be consulting club project applications.
It could be internship applications.
It could be grant applications.
It could be anything that require an approval.

And, you can spend so much time, effort, stress, and worry about your application,
but all those hours just become a few minutes of writing and talking on the other side of the table,
sometimes diligently and other times frivolously.
But at most a few minutes.
And you might make it to the next stage,
but more often or not,
you don't.

## Sunk In Applications

As a kid,
we spent so much time applying to be a club officer,
found a new club,
and college app.
Then in college,
we do the same thing,
with more things to worry about whether it's research or internship.
And I'm sure as adults,
much time is spent on grants, new project proposals, I don't know...

But my point is,
so much time is spent doing applications.

And some roles really don't need that many steps or thousand-words essays.
I feel like at a certain point,
application processes are puffed up to be complicated
so that the position or opportunity in question can appear more enticing and valuable.
It's making it complicated for the sake of appearing worthy.
As another factor,
you might have 1 opportunity with 20 really qualified people looking for it,
so if you are on the other side of the table,
you can every right to raise your application process as hard as you want
as long as 1 person can be expected to pass it.
The difficulty and time-consumingness is not about the true value of the opportunity being applied to,
but the highest bidder's bid in an auction.

Vice versa,
you also have times where,
there's 20 really qualified people,
30 less so,
and 50 stupid people,
but you are hiring 100 people.
So you lower the bar and get them all.
Supply and demand, unfortunately.

## 一句话

Your hours of effort boil down to a few minutes of thinking and a yes or no on the decider.
They might say yes simply because they are in a jolly mood, or vice versa.
A sentence from their mouth is what makes all the difference.
