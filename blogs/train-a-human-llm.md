I'm not an LLM researcher, but
from my understanding, a great LLM agent becomes great because it:

1. Has billions or trillions of parameters
2. Reads a lot of generic texts
3. Reads a lot of domain-specific texts
4. Is then asked to perform domain-specific tasks
5. Fails, observes, thinks, and reflects on the tasks it did
6. And repeats

Seems like we as humans:

1. Have roughly the same amount of brain capacity. And the more we use our brains, the more neurons there are, which means the more we use our brains, the smarter we get
2. Should read a bunch of stuff, both generic and specific
3. And do a lot of stuff
4. And fail
5. And repeat

And that should train a good enough human LLM with great agency.

Oh wait,
the agency part is different for humans.

Machines can tirelessly apply their attention and agency to whatever they're told to do,
but humans have limited energy and focus,
and we don't have infinite drive.

So really,
a better human LLM kinda boils down to better self-drive.
And with that comes more thinking, more doing, more failing, more learning, and repeat.
