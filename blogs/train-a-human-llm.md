I'm not a LLM researcher but,
from my understanding, a great LLM agent becomes great because it:

1. Has billions to trillion of parameters
2. Read a lot of generic texts
3. Read a lot of domain specific texts
4. Was then asked to perform domain specific tasks
5. Fail, observe, think, and reflect the tasks done
6. And repeat

Seems like we as humans:

1. Have roughly the same amount of brain capacity. And the more we use our brain, the more neurons there are, which means the more use our brain, we get smarter
2. Should read a bunch of stuff, generic, and specific
3. And do a lot of stuff
4. And fail
5. And repeat

And that should train a good enough human LLM with great agency.

Oh wait,
the agency part is different for humans.

Because machines can tirelessly span its attention and agency to whatever it's told to,
but humans have limited energy and focus,
and we don't have infinite drive.

So really,
a better human LLM kinda boils down to better self-drive.
