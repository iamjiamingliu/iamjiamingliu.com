## Foreword

I'm NOT a corporate manager.
However, I've been actively involved with student leadership since middle school.
Yes, it's a common place for someone to be involved in student leadership,
but for me, I wasn't just involved: I avidly thought hard about the **fundamental conceptual** behind leadership and
management by reading books, blogs, and comments,
applying what I read to my leadership experiences, and correcting my beliefs from errors.

This blog captures my belief on what managing an organization really boils down to.
Yes, I acknowledge that everything here is backed by my student but not corporate leadership,
but I think the big ideas do carry over.

## The goal

The goal of a leader is:

1. Given a set of resources: human, money, authority, etc.
2. Given a hierarchy of missions, some are one time and others continuous
3. Do whatever it takes to use the above set of resources to fulfill the missions

## The bootstrapping phase

For a new organization, the leader(s) must bootstrap the organization by:

1. Bootstrap the set of resources
    - Recruiting people
    - Secure funding
    - Establish authority if it's a self-bootstrapped endeavor, otherwise it's given to you
2. Define its hierarchy of missions
    - Why does this organization exist? To create value in some area.
    - What kinds of value? Money, convenience, happiness, anything that is good
    - To whom? For corporation, it's the shareholders. For government, it's the citizens. For school clubs, it's the
      club members.
    - The top level mission can be recursively broken down into sub-missions, thus it's a hierarchy of missions.
3. Go from the organization's set of resources to fulfilling its hierarchy of missions. This is a thought decomposition process:
    - What products need to be established? Examples tend to be straightforward:
        - A software product for licensing
        - A merchandise for sale
        - An equipment for rent
        - A club event or a series of club events
    - What services need to be established? Examples can vary here as they are less tangible than products:
        - For social security agency, the service of giving out money after one retires
        - For the military, the service of fighting battles on behalf of its country
        - For restaurants, the service of a nice guest experience: preparing the tables, taking orders, bringing food to
          the table, etc.
        - For clubs, the service of getting a guest speaker to come to the school and seating audience members to listen
          to the guest speaker
    - Design the organization to ensure products and services stay active, so that the organization's mission is
      fulfilled
        - Design the workflows
            - What dependency of steps need to be taken to execute each product and service?
            - In computer science terms, what directed acyclic graphs need to be done for each product and service?
        - Design the organization chart around the workflows, and the chart and the workflows will affect each other
          continuously
            - Look at the workflows. What recurring capabilities come up?
                - For a software company:
                    - Coding?
                    - Getting new customers?
                    - Writing legal paperwork?
                    - Managing finance?
                    - Supporting customers?
                - For a restaurant:
                    - Purchasing ingredients?
                    - Preparing ingredients and cooking?
                    - Serving guests?
                    - Bookkeeping?
            - The design of sub organizations emerge from common capabilities and/or common product/service areas
                - For a software company:
                    - Product development
                    - Sales and marketing
                    - Legal
                    - Treasury
                    - Customer success
                - For restaurants:
                    - Front of the house
                    - Kitchen
                    - Logistics
            - Now we know what sub organizations need to exist to fulfill the organization's mission
            - We assign a subset of resources to this sub organization:
                - People: who is the head of this sub organization, and who are all the people involved
                - Money: budget range. For salary and operational costs
                - Scope / authority: what is this sub organization allowed to do and not allowed to do
            - We assign a subset of workflows, services, and products
                - The people of this sub organization now take ownership of, in part or in full, of workflows defined to fulfill the organization's services, and products
                - The people, coordinated by its head, decompose workflows into concrete roles and tasks
            - Recursive sub-sub organizations may be needed
