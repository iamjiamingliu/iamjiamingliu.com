I joined UCSB ACM as its Co-Head of ICPC branch in my late Sophomore year around January 2025.
After that summer, I've been promoted to its Vice President.
A month later in October 2025,
I was suddenly promoted to its top leadership role as President amidst some leadership shakeout.

As UCSB ACM's President, I had three goals in mind:

1. Restore the rest of the ACM officer team's confidence and trust with top leadership, after the shakeout
2. Improve UCSB ACM's operations efficiency in the officer meetings, logistics, events we host, workshops we run, etc.
3. Take UCSB ACM to the next level in terms of prestige and scope of operations

I accomplished goal 1 and 2 to a decent extent,
but failed to make meaningful progress in taking UCSB ACM to the next level.

Now in May 2026, as I've appointed my succeeding Presidents, which in effect makes me the lame duck,
I think it's a good time to look back at my tenure as UCSB ACM's second generation of President.

## The Grading Scale and My Grade

If I need to grade leaders on a letter grading scale, I would do something like this:

| Letter | Explanation                                                                                                       | Examples using U.S. Presidents or IRL people                  |
|--------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------|
| A      | Founded an organization, or took that organization to the next level, or saved the organization from dying        | George Washington, Franklin D Roosevelt, Abraham Lincoln      |
| B      | Made memorable, but not legendary, improvement or debugging of the organization                                   | Ronald Reagan, Dwight D. Eisenhower                           |
| C      | Smooth sailing, small improvements and small debugging, nothing memorable though                                  | Most corporate managers are like this                         |
| D      | Made the organization worse in some way by taking less action against constant bugs and need for improvements     | Most bureaucrats are like this                                |
| F      | Fucked up the organization beyond resonable repair by a combination of no action, wrong action, and bad character | Actually, very few. Because you need to be really bad to be F |

Great. Now, plug myself into this table. I argue that I would fit in a C+.
By process of elimination, I certainly didn't found ACM or fuck it up beyond reasonable repair, so I'm not A or F.
I spent genuine and timely effort when any issue came up and made more than small improvements, so I'm not D.
I did stabilize the organization's internal confidence and operations after a big leadership shakeout though,
and made many improvements, which I believe would put me on the upper end of C if not B-.
