## 4 Years Later

Many of my friends are graduating from college,
and myself too will finish my last quarter at UCSB by the coming fall or winter.

I look around my friends,
acquaintances,
friends of friends,
and acquaintances of friends,
and I couldn't help but think about this one question:

**We are all human beings, with roughly the same IQ, with exactly 24 hours a day to spare. We are in the same University under the same quality of education. But why, after 3 or 4 years of college, some accomplish little, and some accomplish a lot?**

I look around,
and there's many examples of friends
who entered college with varying level of coding experience,
but have now became super strong engineers in their fields after 4 years.
There's also a few outlier examples of people becoming coding competition aces, ML stars, and actually profitable entrepreneurs.

I look around again,
there's examples of people who earned A's in all the classes they took,
but you certainly wouldn't consider them "great" in any area;
in the sense that,
if you are a manager,
you wouldn't think they would be a competent hire.
And there's also examples of people who had very strong coding experience since high school but plateaued.
Finally,
given all the social media hell and AI tools,
there's ever-increasingly many who just ChatGPT'd their way out of college without any meaningful substance entering their brain,
besides alcohol.

Why such high variance?
Factors like luck, opportunity, and family backgrounds certainly play a role.
But one thing that plays a stronger role and is strictly controlled by one's own will,
is the difference in one's effective management of time.
