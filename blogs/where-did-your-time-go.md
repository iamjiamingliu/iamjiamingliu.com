## 4 Years Later

Many of my friends are graduating from college,
and myself too will finish my last quarter at UCSB by the coming fall or winter.

I look around my friends,
acquaintances,
friends of friends,
and acquaintances of friends,
and I couldn't help but think about this one question:

**We are all human beings, with roughly the same IQ, with exactly 24 hours a day to spare. We are in the same University under the same quality of education. But why, after 3 or 4 years of college, some accomplish little, and some accomplish a lot?**

I look around,
and there's many examples of friends
who entered college with varying level of coding experience,
but have now became super strong engineers in their fields after 4 years.
There's also a few outlier examples of people becoming coding competition aces, ML stars, and actually profitable entrepreneurs.

I look around again,
there's examples of people who earned A's in all the classes they took,
but you certainly wouldn't consider them "great" in any area;
in the sense that,
if you are a manager,
you wouldn't think they would be a competent hire.
And there's also examples of people who had very strong coding experience since high school but plateaued.
Finally,
given all the social media hell and AI tools,
there's ever-increasingly many who just ChatGPT'd their way out of college without any meaningful substance entering their brain,
besides alcohol.

Why such high variance?
Factors like luck, opportunity, and family backgrounds certainly play a role.
But one thing that plays a stronger role and is strictly controlled by one's own will,
is the difference in one's effective management of time.


## A Breakdown of Everyone's 24 Hours

We all have 24 hours a day. Let's break it down.

8 hours goes to sleeping.
For a typical college student, I suspect the sleep duration is 6 hours boosted by much Starbucks and Monster drinks, with small portion consistently below 5 hours.
But still, let's be generous with our budgeting of sleeping time.
The most productive people I know sleep well.

Then, 2 hours goes to chewing food, using the bathroom, and whatever circadian rhythm neccesity requires.
"Dinner time" is chewing food and hanging out with friends, for now, the 2 hours only considers the minimum chewing time.

Thus, with 8 + 2 hours of mandatory survival time budget,
we are left with 14 hours of discretionary time.
Every one of us,
no matter how busy or efficient,
will have the same 14 hours of time per day for their free will.

And this is where different people's budgeting of time diverge in outcome.
Organized by rough stereotypes and ordered by productivity, here's my observations:

**THE ADDICT GAMER**

Skips class, spends at most 2 hours per day on school (ChatGPT of course),
and the remaining 12 hours is almost exclusively given to play PC games.
It shocks me how one's butt can be glued to a chair and eyes be fixed on the monitor without being bored.
Accompanied by this stereotype is their refusal to ever leave their room or interact with any other human beings.
This class of people has zero productivity.

**THE MOST OF US**

Goes to class,
does homework all right,
but during class and outside of class,
their iPhone screen time report averages 6 to 7 hours per day across Instagram, TikTok, etc.
There's gathering with friends alright,
whether it's 3 hours going out or Poker,
but there's no self-driven learning outside of class.
This class of people is the most common type across college students,
and the produtivity is alright during normal times,
with sharp rise during all-nighter final exams studying.

**THE HIGH ACHIEVERS**

Goes to class,
does homework all right,
uses phone for social media and game with 3 to 4 hours a day just as well.
There's gathering with friends alright,
and,
here's the juice:
there's self-learning.
There's time budgeted and spent on learning and practicing materials beyond classroom,
varying from 1 to 4 hours a day, depending on their mood.
The productivity is great,
with push for big project release or paper deadline pumping at maximum productivity for few days a month.

**THE GODS**

I don't know how they do it,
but there's a super tiny class of us who can clear through homework assignments (without ChatGPT) in minutes,
ace the class without paying much attention in lecture,
and curbs much social media and entertainment usage.
There's varying level of social life,
but there's consistently 4 to 8 hours of self-studying (on top of class and all other obligations) on whatever subject they are in every day.
I can't do that, I periodically would study a lot or fall back to resting mode,
but people like this can do that consistently every day and enjoy it.
They are gods.
They are super rare.
They are on max productivity every day.

So here we have 4 stereotypical classes of people.
In practice,
the meaningful distinction exists between **THE MOST OF US** and **THE HIGH ACHIEVERS**
as their numbers are large and there aren't much extreme discipline required
compared to **THE ADDICT GAMER** on one extreme or **THE GODS** on the other.
Now, we can analyze and compare at the breakdown of time spent to infer what's the juice behind differences in achievements.


## The Juice Behind Differences in Achievements

1. Beyond classroom learning
2. Reasonably disciplined entertainment and social time
3. Compounding over time
4. Avoiding useless time commitments
