## Foreword

My journey with tech was not love at first sight.
Having attended Cupertino High School,
which is right next to Apple headquarter and everyone's dad is at some big tech or another,
when I started freshman at CHS,
many people around my age already knew how to import opencv and build flask web apps.

I knew nothing then. I struggled with understanding why a for loop works. I felt super left behind.

My dad, being an iOS engineer his entire life, pushed me to grind out the basics in Python as I was confused on where I was going.
This is the only time in life he pushed me career-wise, and he never pushed me ever since.
The level of non-interference he empowered me with is rare for Asian parents haha.

So from GeeksForGeeks, StackOverflow, and all the goodies before ChatGPT, I learned the basics of Python.

Then, later in freshman year, quarantine hit. I had nothing to do, so I kept playing around with Python.
I coded CLI games, TKinter apps, simulation for rabbit population, web crawlers using BeautifulSoup...
They were so random, but so fun.

With every printed string on the terminal and UI box shown,
I became more and more excited with what I was building.
That became a positive reinforcement loop that still drive me to this day:
learn something, build something with what I learned, and enjoy the beautiful product I built; repeat.

**Learning and building things is the core momentum behind my growth.** Competitive programming and research are sidetracks.

This blog is my review of what I know, what I don't know, how I got here, and what I plan to learn / grow next.

Written on March 1, 2026.

## What I know

1. I'm a hobbyist
2. I can survive an interview
3. I am at the skill of a full-time engineer
4. I am at the skill of a great full-time engineer
5. I can steer an engineering organization in that domain
