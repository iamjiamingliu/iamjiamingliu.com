I'm almost graduated from college now,
but, seeing some underclassmen friends' college admission results just came out,
my scar of college admission outcome unwounded again.

When I was in high school,
Reddit stats comparison, Youtube flexing videos, college counselors, and peers
all seem to concur that,
one's merit in competency would mean better college admission result.
By transitivity, it would mean that colleges that rank higher on the U.S. News would have a class of students
with better merit than colleges with lower rankings,
so the distribution of merit competency would follow a shape like this:

![](/static/img/diagrams/college-expected.png)

The above diagram is what I used to believed about college and students' merits back in high school.
I thought students at colleges with higher ranking have strictly higher merit than students at a lower ranked college.

However, when my own college admission result came out,
as well as hearing the horror stories of several friends and acquaintances around me,
the reality disproved my old beliefs, and now I believe that:

1. Stronger merit in a college applicant doesn't mean better college admission result
2. The strongest students at a lower ranked college have just as much merit, if not more merit, than a good number of students at a higher ranked college

So now, my belief in the distribution of students competency merit across colleges look like this:

![](/static/img/diagrams/college-actual.png)

My new belief implies a couple things:

1. Within the same college, the strongest merited competency could surpass the median by a lot, and the lack of competency could fall under the median by a lot too
2. Across different colleges with various rankings, the strongest students at a lower ranked school could be much stronger than the median competency and higher ranked colleges, and could be just as strong as the top competency at higher ranked colleges too

The variance just amazes me and reminds me once again about the randomness of college admission outcome.
How on earth did these star players get turned away from the big name colleges?
By star players, I mean not only ace of a field like math or coding competition geniuses,
but also well-rounded applicants with strong extracurriculars and leadership too.

I want to end up this essay by offering a few horror stories of friends and acquaintances
whose college admission outcome killed my prior belief in meritocracy of college admission back in their high school:

1. A guy with USACO Gold and then Platinum: UC San Diego
2. Several guys with 4x or more AIME math competition qualifications: UC Santa Cruz, UCSB, etc.
3. A guy with USACO Platinum and (if I remembered correctly) IOI Camp: UCSB
4. A guy with IOI camp: De Anza community college and then UCSD
5. Several guys with super strong and **genuine** extracurriculars (no B.S. non-profit or anything) and leadership experience: UC Santa Cruz, UCSB
6. A guy with top 100 math competition ranking from China: UCSB
7. A guy with USAPHO and USAMO medals: UCSB

It's just crazy to me that folks like these got turned away from big star colleges.
Sure, many of the examples are from UCSB because that's where I am currently attending college,
but still, it just shocks me.
There are many people going to Berkeley EECS or MIT with minor computer science or math experience back in high school,
and you see these people just getting turned away from there even they have such exceptional merit in high school already.

Meritocracy in college admission seems to be gone, so what's the solution?

I don't know. At this point, I don't even know what college admissions are based off of anymore.
Intellectual curiosity? Leadership?
The folks listed above weren't just competition medalists, but also talented in many areas too.
They seem to check many bullet points from an ideal college applicant's checklist,
but still, they got turned away from strong colleges while less experienced people are admitted.

A comforting fact is that, while the folks listed above suffered ill-fated college admission result, they seem to be doing well in life now. I list them in the same order on what they have done after high school and now:

1. Became the best competitor on UCSD ICPC team, scored top 2 in ICPC Southern California regionals in the past 2 years, top 25 in North America Championship, and now he's off to Citadel Securities. He also won 7th place in PICO CTF and top 100 in Putnam math competition
2. Intern at top companies. And Putnam top 250. And ICPC Regionals Qualifiers.
3. Became the best competitor on UCSD ICPC team, made it all the way to ICPC World Finals, beating big schools like UC Berkeley, UCLA, Harvard, etc.
4. Made it to ICPC World Finals in De Anza college, and now (I think) he's going to some quantitative finance company
5. Became a profitable entrepreneur at the age of 20
6. Scored top 10 in the world for Putnam math competition and is considering transferring to MIT
7. Scored top 5 in the world of Putnam math competition and had transferred to MIT

Maybe, over the long run of life, merit and true competency can shine.
Or at least I hope.
